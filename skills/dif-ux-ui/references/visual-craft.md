# Visual craft / 魅力を探して磨く

Use for visual design proposals or an explicit concern about appeal. Scale effort to the request; it is not an extra approval gate or a requirement for every usability fix.

## Define a direction

Record audience, use context, intended feeling, brand traits to preserve and constraints. Replace vague goals such as “stylish” with an interpretable intent: for example, quiet concentration with generous spacing and restrained emphasis. Treat emotional effects as hypotheses until people respond to the work.

If accessible references help, record their provenance and analyze the mechanism: focal hierarchy, proportions, type, spacing, density, material or motion. Separate observed features from inferred effects. Never claim to have researched references that were not inspected. Original directions are acceptable without network access; label them as such.

## Make the alternatives visible

Create two or three directions within the agreed budget on the same representative screen, viewport and content. Vary composition, hierarchy, typography, density or component treatment meaningfully; a recolor is weak evidence of exploration. Include a realistic error or secondary state when it can change the recommendation. Keep required actions, legibility and brand constraints comparable.

| Record for each direction | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Intended impression and audience fit | Explain what the design is trying to achieve |
| Visible choices and recognizable traits | Make the direction concrete and preserve identity |
| Image and its status | Distinguish a proposed mockup from observed UI |
| Strength, sacrifice and uncertainty | Support a choice without a fabricated score |

These visual studies can share an interaction model. They do not satisfy a full proposal's requirement for meaningfully different interaction alternatives on their own. If imagery is unavailable, provide layout specifications and leave visual comparison unverified.

## Critique and refine

Inspect the actual output at intended viewing size, not just its source or prompt. If inspection is unavailable, say so. Choose a provisional direction and refine the most consequential weaknesses; do not infer human adoption. Check:

- **Hierarchy:** where the eye lands, what comes second, and whether competing emphasis distracts from the task.
- **Typography:** size relationships, line length, wrapping, weight and Japanese/English rhythm.
- **Spacing:** alignment, grouping, density and consistent repeated distances.
- **Color and shape:** semantic roles, readability, distinctive features and coherent icons/corners.
- **States and movement:** how feedback remains legible; describe motion as intent unless actually demonstrated.

Use concrete critiques: “Three equally strong buttons obscure the next action” is more useful than “not polished.” Identify what should remain. A visual effect can have a cost: sparse layouts may require more scrolling; compact controls can be harder to tap. Preserve disagreements and unknowns.

## Present for a decision

Lead with the recommendation, show comparable images inline, and use a short tradeoff table. Show before/proposed-after for the refinement. Keep detailed evidence separate and label subjective judgments. Request only the consequential preference if needed; continue reversible exploration while it is unresolved. Adoption, preference, task success and stable readiness remain different claims.
